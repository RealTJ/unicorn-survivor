## Me
What is a vector called that can only be positive and never be negative?

## AI Assistant
In mathematics and linear algebra, the terminology depends on whether the vector's components can be zero:

1. **Non-negative vector**: A vector where all of its components are greater than or equal to zero (they can be positive or zero, but never negative).
2. **Strictly positive vector** (or just **positive vector**): A vector where every single component is strictly greater than zero (no zeros and no negative numbers allowed).

Geometrically, in an $n$-dimensional space, a non-negative vector is said to lie in the **non-negative orthant** (which is the $n$-dimensional equivalent of the first quadrant in a standard 2D graph).

*Note:* If you are thinking about the *length* of a vector rather than its individual components, that is called the **magnitude** (or norm) of the vector. The magnitude is a scalar value (a single number) and is *always* non-negative, regardless of whether the vector's components are positive or negative.
